07 // LEGAL JURISPRUDENCE

Frequently Asked Questions

Common queries regarding the APCSIP-2026 fellowship, digital evidence admissibility, and Indian cyber jurisprudence.

The Amroha Police Cyber Security Internship Program (APCSIP-2026) is an elite state law enforcement initiative conducted by Uttar Pradesh Police in collaboration with Shri Venkateshwara University and Jubilant Bhartia Foundation. It trains selected engineering scholars in digital forensics, OSINT, mobile device acquisition, and cyber jurisprudence under senior police leadership.
Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act prescribes strict statutory conditions for the admissibility of electronic records in court. It certifies that the computer output or digital proof was produced by a computer in lawful control, during ordinary activity, and that the device operated properly without tampering, accompanied by a verified cryptographic hash (MD5/SHA-256).
Call Detail Records (CDR) and Internet Protocol Detail Records (IPDR) provide chronological telecommunication footprints. Investigators parse telecom tower dumps for IMEI/IMSI numbers, cell tower coordinates, call durations, and antenna azimuth angles to triangulate suspect locations and identify co-conspirators in financial and extortion crimes.
The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 establishes explicit boundaries for data processing, consent, and user privacy in India. In legal investigations, law enforcement relies on statutory exceptions (e.g. Section 91 CrPC) while ensuring that digital evidence collection strictly respects constitutional privacy safeguards established in Puttaswamy v. Union of India.
